# Decision — RedWar 1.0-Lite and dual-track Ares

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Governing issue:** #526

## Context

The current Ares programme mixes two different product needs:

1. an Ares that is reliable enough to play against and to act as a controlled agent inside balance experiments;
2. an Ares that has completed the project's competitive/scientific strength programme.

The second requirement can require repeated search/evaluation hypotheses, Arena experiments, methodological corrections and substantial CI/evidence work. A rejected experiment is valuable research, but it should not by itself prevent the first playable local RedWar release.

The balance research also establishes an important methodological correction: **Ares is not the Balance Lab**. Ares is an important experimental agent inside the Balance Lab, but hero value must not be reduced to a single Ares win rate or a single aggregate statistic. fileciteturn176file0L2-L2

## Decision

RedWar will have a **1.0-Lite local release path** while the competitive Ares programme remains open.

The product uses a frozen **Ares Balance Baseline**. The baseline is an execution/reference configuration for controlled balance work, not a claim that the Ares is balanced, optimal or competitively proven.

The baseline is considered adequate for 1.0-Lite only when it is:

- correct and regression-clean;
- reliable in legal action generation and state transitions;
- reliably terminating with no known hangs in representative matches;
- deterministic/reproducible under a fixed configuration;
- stable and documented at each supported difficulty level;
- frozen by exact engine revision, ruleset, search budget, seed policy and experimental population/context;
- capable of producing complete, provenance-valid evidence for the Balance Lab under the declared protocol.

### Balance evidence requirements

The Lite baseline does **not** make aggregate hero win rate an accepted balance measure.

For balance decisions, the Balance Lab must preserve and analyse context such as:

```text
hero
× position
× allied composition
× opponent / matchup
× initiative / colour
× ruleset
× seed
× Ares policy / player-skill context
× outcome / terminal reason
```

The minimum methodological pattern is:

```text
controlled baseline
→ matched games / seeds / colours
→ selection + provenance audit
→ contextual matchup / composition evidence
→ candidate intervention
→ independent hold-out validation
```

Where the question is colour or first-player balance, calibration and hold-out sets must be separated; the same observations used to choose compensation must not be reused as independent confirmation. fileciteturn183file0L2-L2

Balance interventions should normally begin with coarse-to-fine **integer cost** changes, then escalate to the smallest hero-specific mechanic intervention justified by the observed defect. Candidate changes must be evaluated for ecosystem effects, matchup/counter structure and contextual behaviour, not only the changed hero's marginal win rate. fileciteturn178file0L2-L2 fileciteturn181file0L2-L2

The legacy `tools/balance/auto_pricer.py` may remain as diagnostic/telemetry tooling, but it is **not a balance authority** and must not by itself justify a price, mechanic or roster change. Its aggregate residual heuristic does not model hero × position × composition × matchup interactions or global metagame effects. fileciteturn181file0L2-L2

Balance statistics remain descriptive and conditional on the declared baseline, population and protocol. They are not proof of intrinsic hero power, global game balance or competitive Ares strength. Hero strength is explicitly contextual rather than a fixed scalar. fileciteturn179file0L2-L2

## Consequences

- 1.0-Lite does not require closing #372.
- Competitive Ares improvements do not automatically replace the product baseline.
- Replacing the product baseline requires revalidation of balance evidence that depends on it.
- NNUE and further search optimisation remain optional research for 1.0-Lite.
- The local product can be developed without accounts, matchmaking, server infrastructure or competitive ranking.
- The Balance Lab remains a separate methodological layer; Ares is one experimental agent within it.
- Legacy heuristic pricing tooling is not promoted to authoritative balance logic.
- #372 remains responsible for controlled strength evidence, Arena promotion and long-term Ares competitiveness.

## Non-goals

This decision does not:

- weaken the Arena promotion protocol;
- change promotion thresholds;
- alter game rules;
- declare current Ares strength sufficient for competitive promotion;
- declare hero prices balanced from aggregate win-rate telemetry;
- turn Ares into a substitute for the Balance Lab.
